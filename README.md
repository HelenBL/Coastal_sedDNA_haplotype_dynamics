# Long-term haplotype dynamics from coastal sedimentary DNA

Sedimentary ancient DNA (sedaDNA) provides a temporal record of past biological communities and offers the possibility of examining within-species sequence variation through time.

In this study, we analysed dated sediment cores from eight coastal wetlands across the Atlantic and Mediterranean regions of the Iberian Peninsula using COI and 18S metabarcoding. Amplicon sequence variants (ASVs) were treated as putative haplotypes to investigate:

- long-term occurrence of native (NAT), non-indigenous (NIS) and cryptogenic (CRY) taxa;
- haplotype richness, dominance and spatial distribution;
- differences in haplotype patterns recovered with COI and 18S;
- changes in haplotype composition through time; and
- temporal turnover in haplotype composition across the sedimentary records.

The analyses show that within-species sequence variation preserved in sedimentary archives has both spatial and temporal dimensions. Haplotype composition changed through time, with some variants repeatedly detected across periods and others restricted to particular intervals.
